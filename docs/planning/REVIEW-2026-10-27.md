# Principal review and delivery — 27 October 2026

Scope: a fresh engineering, design, strategy and investment-committee review of the existing project, carried through to implementation. It reconciles with the 56-task backlog; it does not replace it. No chain write, token spend, contract deployment, faucet request, customer contact, purchase or paid service occurred. Google Cloud was not touched.

## Problems found

1. **The public story overclaimed or confused.** A dark "fund working assets, follow the income" marketplace page, "Promised minimum", "A little ownership starts here", and an unexplained sold-out dead end, over a product whose purchase money is not escrowed.
2. **No reviewer journey.** A judge had to assemble evidence from docs. There was no page listing the contract, the recorded transactions, what they prove, and the criteria.
3. **Business case did not survive its own arithmetic.** At 1% / 0.5% an operator must deposit about $240,000 a year to cover its service cost (assumed inputs, `docs/FINANCIAL-MODEL.md`).
4. **Funding half of the product is the weakest-protected part.** `create` is permissionless; `buy` pays the operator directly; nothing controls spending, verifies assets or revenue.
5. **Missing submission items:** no `LICENSE` file though the README said MIT; no demo video; no pitch deck; the office-hour deadline had passed.
6. **First-paint and readability.** Text as small as 8–10 px; hero faded for seconds while the chain client initialised on the main thread; several states left unreadable contrast after the palette change (found and fixed, below).

## Direction chosen and why

Narrow the first product to **operator revenue-sharing administration**; keep funding as an optional, test-only module. Basis: incumbents that work control collections, verification or a legal wrapper (`MARKET-AND-COMPETITORS.md`); the only comparable software precedent sells administration to the sponsor; and the payout path is the part the contract actually enforces. This is a positioning decision. Core v1's rights, terms and economics are unchanged.
